It doesn't read the news

It doesn't have an inbox. Your robot ISA never opens a newsletter, a press release or a tip. Every Sunday evening it pulls the week's prices, applies the same rules it was tested on, and proposes its orders. You approve by Monday lunchtime and they go in at the open. One ISA. Three jobs. The robot does all three.

The foundation

Most of your money goes into one low-cost fund that tracks the world's markets. Nothing to pick, nothing to watch. The robot keeps it topped up and in balance.

The hunter

A slice rides the market's strongest stocks while they're running and steps out by rule when they stop. The robot finds them, sizes them and sets every exit before it buys. You tap Approve.

The moonshot

A small pot the robot keeps for the biggest swings: the breakouts it spots, or the one bet you want to make yourself. Either way the size is capped and the exit is set in advance, so a miss can't dent the rest.

Built for people with a day job

You see the stock everyone's talking about at lunchtime. By the time you've finished work it's old news, and you've no time to sit watching charts anyway. So you do nothing, and the ISA allowance sits there.

A robot ISA is the other way round. You set the rules once: what it's allowed to buy, how much risk you'll take, when it should sell. Every Sunday evening it checks the market, does the maths and puts the proposed moves on your phone. You tap approve by Monday lunchtime, with a reminder if you forget. No chart-watching, no chasing, no deciding at midnight.

It won't catch every runaway stock and it doesn't promise to beat anything. It makes sure your plan actually runs while you get on with your life. Capital at risk: it can lose money as well as make it.

The ISA rules change in April 2027

From 6 April 2027, if you're under 65, only £12,000 of your £20,000 ISA allowance can go into cash. To use the other £8,000 tax-free, it has to be invested, in a stocks and shares ISA (or up to £4,000 in a Lifetime ISA, if you qualify).

Now (2026/27)

Up to £20,000 a year in a cash ISA.

Money can move from a stocks and shares ISA into cash.

From 6 April 2027, under 65

Up to £12,000 in cash. The other £8,000 only stays tax-free if it's invested.

Moving money from a stocks and shares ISA into cash is banned.

Most people have only ever used a cash ISA. If that's you, April brings a new question: what to do with the £8,000. My Robot I.S.A. opens in January, so there's time to set it up, practise on paper money for a few weeks and make up your own mind before the new tax year starts.

Over-65s keep the £20,000 cash limit, and the total allowance stays £20,000 until April 2031. Investing can lose money; cash in a cash ISA can't fall in value. Announced in the November 2025 Budget (GOV.UK); details can change before they take effect.

Why it lives in an ISA

In a stocks and shares ISA you pay no Capital Gains Tax and no tax on dividends. You can put in up to £20,000 each tax year.
