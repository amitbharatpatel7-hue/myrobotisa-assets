It doesn't read the news

It doesn't have an inbox. Your robot ISA never opens a newsletter, a press release or a tip. Every Sunday it pulls the week's prices, applies the same rules it was tested on, and at 7pm its proposed orders land in your inbox. You approve by 2pm on Monday; approved orders go straight to Trading 212 and fill when the market opens. The only research it ever did was the backtest, on years of data, before a pound moved. Nothing anyone emails you at 7pm can change its mind.

One ISA. Three jobs. The robot does all three.

The foundation

In the default settings, most of the money goes into one low-cost fund that tracks the world's markets. Nothing to pick, nothing to watch. The robot invests new cash and keeps it in balance.

The hunter

A slice rides the market's strongest stocks while they're running and steps out by rule when they stop. The robot finds them, sizes them and sets the exit rule before it buys. You tap Approve.

The moonshot

A small pot the robot keeps for the biggest swings: the breakouts it spots, or the one bet you want to make yourself. Either way the size is capped and the exit rule is set in advance, to limit what a miss can cost.

Built for people with a day job

You see the stock everyone's talking about at lunchtime. By the time you've finished work it's old news, and you've no time to sit watching charts anyway. So you do nothing, and the ISA allowance sits there.

A robot ISA is the other way round. You set the rules once: what it's allowed to buy, how much risk you'll take, when it should sell. Every Sunday at 7pm it puts the week's proposed moves on your phone, the maths already done. You tap approve by 2pm on Monday, with a reminder if you forget. No chart-watching, no chasing, no deciding at midnight.

It won't catch every runaway stock and it doesn't promise to beat anything. It makes sure your plan actually runs while you get on with your life. Capital at risk: it can lose money as well as make it.

Three words explained

ISA: an account where everything your money makes is tax-free. Anyone in the UK over 18 can put in up to £20,000 a year.

Stocks and shares ISA: the kind of ISA that buys shares and funds, so its value goes up and down. A cash ISA is a savings account.

Pension or SIPP: tax relief on the way in, but locked until 55 (57 from April 2028). My Robot I.S.A. doesn't work with pensions yet. Why not?

The ISA rules change in April 2027

From 6 April 2027, if you're under 65, only £12,000 of your £20,000 ISA allowance can go into cash. The other £8,000 can still go in tax-free, into a stocks and shares ISA, an Innovative Finance ISA or, if you qualify, a Lifetime ISA (up to £4,000).

Now (2026/27)

Up to £20,000 a year in a cash ISA.

Money can move from a stocks and shares ISA into cash.

From 6 April 2027, under 65

Up to £12,000 in cash. The other £8,000 only stays tax-free if it's invested.

Moving money from a stocks and shares ISA into cash is banned.

Many people have only ever used a cash ISA. If that's you, April brings a new question: what to do with the £8,000. My Robot I.S.A. opens in January, so there's time to set it up, practise on paper money for a few weeks and make up your own mind before the new tax year starts.

Over-65s keep the £20,000 cash limit, and the total allowance stays £20,000 until April 2031. Investing can lose money; cash in a cash ISA can't fall in value. Announced in the November 2025 Budget (GOV.UK); details can change before they take effect.

Questions people ask

Why is it tested from 2020 and not further back?

Because those years cover almost every kind of market in under seven years:

  • the Covid crash in 2020
  • the 2021 boom
  • the 2022 bear market, when interest rates went up
  • the 2023–24 recovery
  • the April 2025 tariff drop

Sharp falls, recoveries and long rises are what a test needs to put the rules through. A backtest checks that the rules behave sensibly in all of them; it doesn't predict what happens next.

Is a robot placing orders allowed on Trading 212?

Yes. Trading 212's API terms allow a program to propose orders as long as a person confirms each one, and Trading 212 confirmed this to me in writing in October 2026. That's exactly how the robot works: it proposes, you tap Approve, and only then does an order reach your account. My Robot I.S.A. isn't affiliated with Trading 212.

How do the updates work, and what happens after a year?

Your price includes 12 months of updates: fixes, new brokers and improvements, delivered to licensed buyers through the private repo. After the first year, updates are £99 a year, whether you paid the £99 founding price or £199. It never renews automatically; we email you when your year is up and you choose.

If you stop, you keep every lesson and video released while you had updates, and the version of the code you have is yours forever. It doesn't switch off, though a broker changing its systems may need an update to keep working.

Can I cancel after buying?

You have the legal 14-day right to cancel until you first open the code. At checkout you'll be asked to agree that opening the code ends that right, because once it's opened it can't be handed back. Cancelling ends lesson access and updates. None of this affects your rights if the code or lessons turn out to be faulty.

Can I use my pension or SIPP?

Not yet. The robot places orders through Trading 212's connection for programs, and Trading 212 only opens that connection for Stocks and Shares ISA and Invest accounts. Trading 212 launched a SIPP in 2026, but programs can't connect to it. If that changes, we'll test it before we say yes.

Please don't move a pension to use My Robot I.S.A. Moving a pension can lose you valuable benefits, and it's worth getting regulated financial advice first.

Which brokers does it work with?

Trading 212 at launch, in a Stocks and Shares ISA or an Invest account. More brokers to come. Let us cook, and we'll serve them once they're tested.

Do I need to be able to code?

No. The 14 lessons assume no coding experience, and the QuickStart gets you to your first paper trade in about 30 minutes. If you've done some coding before, you can skip ahead.

Does it use real money straight away?

No. It starts on Trading 212's practice account with pretend money. Going live is a deliberate switch you make later, and even then nothing is bought or sold until you approve it.

How much time does it take each week?

About 30 minutes to set up, then a few minutes a week: proposals land at 7pm on Sunday, and you approve them by 2pm on Monday from your phone.
