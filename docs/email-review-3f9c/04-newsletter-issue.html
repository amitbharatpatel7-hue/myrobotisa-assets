The L: day one

The very first run proposed 11 orders. Trading 212 turned down all 11. We were sending share quantities to six decimal places, and it accepts four.

The lesson: it was fixed the next day with a check that rounds every order to what the broker accepts before it's sent. It happened on pretend money, which is exactly what practice is for, and that check now ships in every copy of the robot.

Weeks two to four: quietly doing its job

Two weeks where the account already matched its rules, so the robot checked everything and left it alone. Then the first monthly rebalance: six orders to bring the mix back on target, all six accepted.

8 October: real money

After four practice weeks, our founder switched the robot on in his own ISA. Going live is a setting you change yourself, when you're ready, never something the robot decides for you.

Live week one: a proper test

Its first live week was a falling one for chip shares. The robot did exactly what it was built to do: no panic, no 3am decisions, every rule followed.

13 orders went to Trading 212 and 12 were accepted. The 13th was turned down because the cash had already gone on the others, so the robot put it back in Sunday's 7pm plan, where it was approved from a phone.
