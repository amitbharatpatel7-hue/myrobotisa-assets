Questions people ask

Why is it tested from 2020 and not further back?

Because those years cover almost every kind of market in under seven years:

  • the Covid crash in 2020
  • the 2021 boom
  • the 2022 bear market, when interest rates went up
  • the 2023–24 recovery
  • the April 2025 tariff drop

Sharp falls, recoveries and long rises are what a test needs to put the rules through. A backtest checks that the rules behave sensibly in all of them; it doesn't predict what happens next.

If it doesn't read the news, how does it cope with another pandemic or a crash?

Better than reading the news would, and that's the point. In a crash the headlines are loudest exactly when they're least useful: by the time a story breaks, prices have already moved, and reacting to it is how people end up selling low and buying back high. The robot never sees a headline, so it can't panic and it can't chase. It watches prices, where every crisis shows up first, and follows rules that were set calmly, in advance:

  • Falling holdings are sold by rule. Each one has a trailing stop under its recent peak and is sold if it closes below its 40-week average, so a fund that drops out of its uptrend is swapped for cash instead of ridden to the bottom.
  • No buying into a fall. New positions need a price above that same average, so in a slide the robot holds cash rather than guessing where the bottom is.
  • A circuit breaker. If the whole account falls a set amount below its best-ever value (15% on the Balanced profile, and you can change it), all trading stops until you choose to restart.
  • You still decide. Every order waits for your approval, so if the news worries you, you simply don't tap approve.

The Covid crash is in the backtest for this reason. Honest limits: no system can see a crash coming, and the robot checks once a week, so a fall on a Tuesday is acted on that Sunday. What you get is a plan that's already made before the panic starts. Capital at risk.

Is a robot placing orders allowed on Trading 212?

Yes. Trading 212's API terms allow a program to propose orders as long as a person confirms each one, and Trading 212 confirmed this to us in writing in October 2026. That's exactly how the robot works: it proposes, you tap Approve, and only then does an order reach your account. My Robot I.S.A. isn't affiliated with Trading 212.

Your price includes 12 months of updates: fixes, new brokers and improvements, delivered to licensed buyers through the private repo. After the first year, updates are £99 a year, whether you paid the £99 founding price or £199, and founding members keep that £99 rate for as long as they want updates. It never renews automatically; we email you when your year is up and you choose.

If you stop, you keep every lesson and video released while you had updates, and the version of the code you have is yours forever. It doesn't switch off, though a broker changing its systems may need an update to keep working.

Two ways. The QuickStart promise: if you can't get to your first paper trade within 60 minutes with our help, email within 14 days of buying and we refund you in full. And the legal 14-day right to cancel, which lasts until you first open the code. At checkout you'll be asked to agree that opening the code ends that right, because once it's opened it can't be handed back. Cancelling ends lesson access and updates. None of this affects your rights if the code or lessons turn out to be faulty.

Not yet. The robot places orders through Trading 212's connection for programs, and Trading 212 only opens that connection for Stocks and Shares ISA and Invest accounts. Trading 212 launched a SIPP in 2026, but programs can't connect to it. If that changes, we'll test it before we say yes, and we'll announce it on our socials first and then in the newsletter: @myrobotisa on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube.

No. The 14 lessons assume no coding experience, and the QuickStart gets you to your first paper trade in about 30 minutes. If you've done some coding before, you can skip ahead.

Does it use real money straight away?

No. It starts on Trading 212's practice account with pretend money. Going live is a deliberate switch you make later, and even then nothing is bought or sold until you approve it.

How much time does it take each week?

About 30 minutes to set up. After that, less than a minute a week: the proposals land at 7pm on Sunday with the reason for each one, and approving them is a tap on your phone, or a click on your Mac or PC, any time before 2pm on Monday. Reading the reasons takes as long as you want it to.

Can I share the code with friends and family?

Each robot belongs to one person, like the ISA it runs in: a friend's robot needs their own Trading 212 account, their own keys and their own settings, so a copy of yours wouldn't work for them anyway. Your copy is licensed to you alone and carries your own ID, which keeps your updates and support coming.

What you can do, and please do: show them your dashboard, talk about it as much as you like, and send them to the free test drive and the free newsletter. If they want their own robot, they buy their own copy and get their own private repo, lessons and support.

What happens if I miss the 2pm Monday deadline?

Nothing. The proposals expire and no order is placed. There's a reminder on Monday morning and another at midday, and if you still don't approve, the robot simply proposes again the following Sunday. Missing a week never puts you in a worse position than doing nothing would.

What can the robot do to my account, and what can't it?

Your money stays in your own Trading 212 ISA, in your name. The robot's key can read your holdings and place the orders you approve. It can't withdraw money, change your bank details or log in to your account, and you can revoke the key in the Trading 212 app at any moment. Your app uses a separate read-only key, so even if someone got into your dashboard they couldn't trade.

Does it cost anything to run, and does my computer need to be on?

No computer needed: the robot runs on GitHub's servers on a schedule and the approvals happen in your app, on your phone or computer. GitHub's free plan covers the few minutes of computing it uses each week, Trading 212 charges no commission, and the email it sends sits in a free tier. Claude, which the lessons use as your build partner, has a free tier that's enough for the course. The bonus toolkit lists every tool we use and which are free.

How much money do I need to start?

None to begin: it starts on practice money. When you go live there's no minimum from us, and Trading 212 sells fractions of shares, so the robot can work with a few hundred pounds. Start with an amount you'd be comfortable seeing fall, and add to it as your confidence grows. Capital at risk.

Yes. One settings file holds the risk mix, the limits and what it's allowed to trade, and you can change any of them at any time. Day 4 walks through changing the settings, then the code itself, and day 5 shows you how to test your own rules on past data before any money moves.

How seriously do you take security?

Seriously enough that the robot is built so a mistake can't cost you more than a bad trade:

  • Two keys, never one. Your app uses a read-only broker key; the key that places orders lives only in your GitHub's encrypted secrets and never leaves there. A test in the code fails if the read-only key ever gains the power to trade.
  • It can't touch your money. Trading 212's connection for programs can place and check orders. It can't withdraw money, change bank details or log in to your account, and you can revoke the key in the Trading 212 app at any moment.
  • Your dashboard sits behind your own login. Nobody at My Robot I.S.A. can see it, and we never hold your keys or your account.
  • Limits and an off switch. Position limits, sanity checks on every order, and a halt you can hit from your phone or computer that stops everything in one move. Day 12 of the lessons is about exactly this.
  • Nothing to steal from us. We hold your email address and your approvals, stored with Supabase in London, and we never sell them. We don't hold your money, your keys or your account.

We're working towards Cyber Essentials certification before launch, and the security checks in the code ship with every copy, so your robot is tested the same way ours is.
